        Four regions of the H-R diagram are labelled P, Q, R and S. Stars like the Sun fuse hydrogen in their cores. In which region are these stars found?

        22 marks

        The proton–proton chain in the core of a star has three stages.
        Stage 1: ¹₁H + ¹₁H → ²₁H + ⁰₊₁e + ν
        Stage 2: ²₁H + ¹₁H → ³₂He + Z
        Stage 3: ³₂He + ³₂He → X + ¹₁H + ¹₁H

        Identify the particles or radiation represented by X and Z.2

        Two stars have the same surface temperature. The radius of star P is three times the radius of star Q. What is the luminosity of P divided by the luminosity of Q?

        The demand of the real test — one rearrangement, one conversion or one intermediate step.

        56 marks

        Barnard's Star is a red dwarf on the main sequence. It is one of the closest stars to the Sun. Its surface temperature is 3130 K and its luminosity is 1.35 × 10²⁴ W.

        (a) Calculate the power per unit area emitted from the surface of Barnard's Star.3

        (b) Calculate the radius of Barnard's Star.3

        65 marks

        Aldebaran is an orange giant in the constellation Taurus. It is 65 ly from Earth. It has a radius of 3.1 × 10¹⁰ m. Its apparent brightness is 3.6 × 10⁻⁸ W m⁻².

        Determine the surface temperature of Aldebaran.5

        72 marks

        Canopus is the second-brightest star in the night sky. It is 2.9 × 10¹⁸ m from Earth. Its apparent brightness is 3.9 × 10⁻⁸ W m⁻².

        Show that the luminosity of Canopus is 4.1 × 10³⁰ W. Write your working in your jotter.2

        Push

        7 marks

        Two or more steps, an unfamiliar context, and a judgement to justify.

        83 marks

        A group of pupils are talking about the stars Capella and Regulus.

        • Pupil 1: "Why does Regulus look blue-white, while Capella looks yellow?"
        • Pupil 2: "Capella looks brighter than Regulus, so it must be closer to us."
        • Pupil 3: "Two stars that look close together in the sky must be close together in space."
        • Pupil 4: "A star's colour tells you nothing about its temperature."
        Use your knowledge of physics to comment on the discussion.

        93 marks

        Every star ends its life as a white dwarf, a neutron star or a black hole. Using your knowledge of physics, comment on the similarities and differences in how stars evolve into these three objects.

        101 mark

        Proxima Centauri is a small red dwarf. Stars like it stay on the main sequence for thousands of billions of years.

        Scientists know the mass of stars like this. Suggest why they have still not observed what happens to these stars at the end of their lives.1

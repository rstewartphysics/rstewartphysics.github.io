The idea

The wire carries a current through the magnet's field. The field pushes on the wire:

F = IlB sin θ

The wire is at 90° to the field, so sin θ = 1 and F = IlB.

The wire pushes back on the magnet with an equal and opposite force (Newton's third law). The magnet sits on the balance, so the reading changes by Δm:

F = Δm g

Change I. Keep l the same. A graph of F against I is a straight line through the origin, with gradient Bl. So:

B = gradient ÷ l

  • F force on the wire (N)
  • B magnetic induction (T)
  • I current in the wire (A)
  • θ angle between wire and field
  • l length of wire in the field (m)
  • Δm change in balance reading (kg)
  • g 9.8 N kg⁻¹

Aim and set-up

Aim: to measure the magnetic induction B between the poles of a magnet.

Predict before you start: sketch the graph of F against I you expect, and say why. It goes in your write-up, under your aim.

Equipment

Safety

  • Never more than 3.0 A. Start with the variable resistor at its largest resistance and the supply turned right down.
  • The wire gets hot. Close the switch only while you take a reading. Do not touch the wire while current flows, or just after.
  • Keep the magnet clear of the balance electronics. The magnet sits on the wooden block, never straight on the pan. Keep it away from phones and bank cards.
  • Magnets snap together hard. Keep your fingers clear when you move them.

Method

Tick each step as you do it.

Reading goes negative? The force on the magnet is upwards. That is fine: record the size of Δm and ignore the minus sign.

If it goes wrong

The reading does not change.
Check the ammeter shows a current. Check the wire runs through the gap between the magnets, not over the top of them.
The reading creeps up or down.
The wire is warming up. Open the switch, let it cool, tare again, and take each reading quickly.
The reading jumps about.
Something is touching. Check the wire and the leads do not touch the magnet, the yoke or the balance, and nothing leans on the bench.

Finding B

  1. Mean and random uncertainty. For each current, find the mean Δm. Random uncertainty = (largest − smallest) ÷ number of readings.
  2. Uncertainty in each mean. Combine the balance's scale reading, calibration and random uncertainties: ΔW = √(ΔX² + ΔY² + ΔZ²). Leave out any uncertainty smaller than a third of the biggest one.
  3. Force. F = Δm g, with Δm in kg (divide grams by 1000) and g = 9.8 N kg⁻¹. The uncertainty in F = (uncertainty in Δm, in kg) × 9.8.
  4. Graph. Plot F (N) up the side against I (A) along the bottom. Add error bars for F. If the ammeter's uncertainty is big enough to see, add error bars for I too. Draw the line of best fit. It should pass close to the origin.
  5. Gradient. Use two points on your line, far apart (not data points). Gradient = rise ÷ run, in N A⁻¹.
  6. B. B = gradient ÷ l, with l in metres. The unit is the tesla (T).
  7. Uncertainty in the gradient. Draw the steepest and the shallowest lines that still pass through every error bar. Uncertainty = (steepest − shallowest) ÷ 2. LINEST in Excel gives the same thing.
  8. Uncertainty in B. Combine the fractional uncertainties:

    ΔB / B = √( (Δgradient / gradient)² + (Δl / l)² )

    Round the uncertainty to one significant figure. Your answer: B = ( … ± … ) T.

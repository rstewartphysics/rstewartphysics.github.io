Core Notes (keep it simple)

These notes match the main success-criteria headings in your Electricity 2 booklet/KO (potential dividers → LEDs → sensors → control → switching → soldering). :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

1) Potential dividers (the big idea)

A potential divider is two resistors in series across a supply. The output is the voltage at the junction (often “across R₂”). :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

Vs + ──[ R1 ]───●───[ R2 ]─── 0V
               │
              Vout

Equation: Vout = (R2 / (R1 + R2)) × Vs. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

  • If R₂ increases (R₁ fixed), Vout increases.
  • If R₁ increases (R₂ fixed), Vout decreases.

2) Diodes and LEDs

  • A diode lets current flow in one direction only. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}
  • An LED is a diode that emits light when forward biased. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}
  • An LED needs a protective resistor in series to limit current. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}
+Vs ──[ R ]───|>|─── 0V

How to calculate R:

  • VR = Vs − VLED
  • R = VR / I (I in amps)
  • Choose the next highest standard resistor (safer). :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}

3) Special components (sensors + capacitor)

  • LDR: more light → resistance decreases. :contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8}
  • Thermistor (NTC): higher temperature → resistance decreases. :contentReference[oaicite:9]{index=9}
  • Capacitor: stores charge; often used for timing or smoothing.

4) Control circuits (4 types)

You’ll meet four “environment → voltage change” setups: low light, high light, low temperature, high temperature. :contentReference[oaicite:10]{index=10}

  • They are essentially sensor dividers arranged so the output goes the way you want.
  • In class/practical you measure the voltage under two conditions (e.g. bright/dim). :contentReference[oaicite:11]{index=11}

5) Switching (transistor / MOSFET idea)

  • A transistor or MOSFET can act as an electronic switch. :contentReference[oaicite:12]{index=12}
  • You increase the control voltage until the LED just begins to light — that’s the switching voltage idea used in the booklet practical. :contentReference[oaicite:13]{index=13}
  • The variable resistor is used to set the threshold for the light/temperature condition. :contentReference[oaicite:14]{index=14}

6) Practical habits (reduces faults)

  • Pre-power checks: correct supply value, wiring continuity, correct resistor values. :contentReference[oaicite:15]{index=15}
  • Functionality checks: does LED operate, measure voltages, measure current, confirm within limits. :contentReference[oaicite:16]{index=16}
  • Soldering tools: track cutter (break strips), heat sink (protect components), solder sucker (remove solder). :contentReference[oaicite:17]{index=17}

✅ Core Notes (open only what you need)

🧾 Key Equations (the only ones you need here)

Learn these and you can do most of the calculations in this unit:

V = I·R V2 = ( R2 / (R1 + R2) ) × Vs RLED = (Vs − Vf) / I (I in amps)
  • Vs = supply voltage.
  • V2 is the voltage across R2 (same as Vout).
  • For LEDs, use I in amps (e.g. 10 mA = 0.010 A).
🔁 Circuit Rules Recap (from Electricity 1)
  • Series: current is the same everywhere, voltages add up.
  • Parallel: voltage is the same across each branch, current splits.
  • Used mainly for divider/control circuits here, but the rules still apply.
📏 Measuring in Circuits (meters)
  • Ammeter: measure current → connect in series.
  • Voltmeter: measure voltage → connect in parallel.
  • Ohmmeter: measure resistance → power OFF.
  • In divider questions, you’ll often measure V2 across R2.
🔢 Potential Dividers (the core idea)
  • Two resistors in series create a smaller output voltage.
  • Output is usually V2 (across R2).
  • If R2 increases (R1 fixed), then V2 increases.
  • If R1 increases (R2 fixed), then V2 decreases.
🔧 Components & What They Do
  • Voltage rails: +V and 0V lines that power the circuit.
  • Resistor: limits current / shares voltage.
  • Variable resistor: adjustable resistance.
  • SPDT switch: middle contact connects to one of two outputs.
  • Diode: current one direction only.
  • LED: light-emitting diode — needs a series resistor.
  • Capacitor: stores charge (used for timing/delay with a resistor).
  • LDR: more light → lower resistance.
  • Thermistor (NTC): higher temp → lower resistance.
  • Transistor (NPN BJT): electronic switch (current-controlled).
  • MOSFET (n-channel enhancement): electronic switch (voltage-controlled).
🌡️ Sensors (LDRs & Thermistors)
  • Sensors are often one resistor in a potential divider.
  • LDR: brighter → lower resistance.
  • NTC thermistor: hotter → lower resistance.
  • Environment changes resistance → V2 changes → circuit responds.
🎛️ Control Circuits (the 4 you must recognise)

Aim: make V2 go high when you want the output to switch on. Remember: V2 increases when R2 increases (R1 fixed).

  • Low light (LDR): LDR is R2 → low light = high R → V2 high.
  • High light (LDR): LDR is R1 → high light = low R → V2 high.
  • Low temp (NTC): NTC is R2 → low temp = high R → V2 high.
  • High temp (NTC): NTC is R1 → high temp = low R → V2 high.
🔄 Switching & Control (BJT / MOSFET as a switch)
  • NPN BJT: starts turning on when VBE ≈ 0.7 V.
  • Use a base resistor to limit base current.
  • MOSFET: gate voltage controls switching (tiny gate current).
  • Divider + sensor creates the control voltage for switching.
💡 Diodes & LEDs (safe LED circuits)
  • Diodes/LEDs work in the forward direction only.
  • LED needs a series resistor to prevent damage.
  • Use: VR = Vs − Vf, then R = VR / I.
🧰 Practical Skills (stripboard & soldering)
  • Tracks run in lines — add track cuts to stop unwanted connections.
  • Heat sink protects heat-sensitive parts.
  • Solder sucker removes mistakes.
  • Good joint: shiny, secure, not a blob.
🔍 Fault Finding (what to do when it doesn’t work)
  • Change one thing at a time, test again.
  • Check: rails, polarity, correct resistors.
  • Stripboard: track cuts, solder bridges, shorting legs.
  • Measure: Vs → V2 → output.

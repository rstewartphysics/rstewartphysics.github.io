✅ Core Notes (open only what you need)

🧾 Key Equations (the only ones you need here)

Learn these and you can do most of the calculations in this unit:

V = I·R Vout = ( R2 / (R1 + R2) ) × Vs RLED = (Vs − Vf) / I (I in amps)
  • Vout is normally taken across R2 in this unit.
  • For LEDs, use I in amps (e.g. 10 mA = 0.010 A).
🔢 Potential Dividers (the core idea)
  • A potential divider uses two resistors in series to create a smaller output voltage.
  • We usually measure Vout at the junction and treat it as the voltage across R2.
  • If R2 increases (R1 fixed), then Vout increases.
  • If R1 increases (R2 fixed), then Vout decreases.
  • Dividers are used so a circuit can produce a control voltage (for example for a transistor base).
💡 Diodes & LEDs (safe LED circuits)
  • LEDs and diodes only work in the forward direction.
  • An LED must have a series resistor to limit current and prevent damage.
  • Method: find voltage across resistor: VR = Vs − Vf, then use R = VR / I.
  • In real builds, choose the next standard resistor value up for safety.
🌡️ Sensors (LDRs & Thermistors)
  • Sensors are often used as one resistor in a potential divider.
  • LDR: brighter light → lower resistance → divider output changes.
  • NTC thermistor: higher temperature → lower resistance → divider output changes.
  • The key story: environment changes resistance → Vout changes → circuit responds.
🔄 Switching & Control (transistor as a switch)
  • A transistor can be used as an electronic switch to turn an output on/off.
  • In this course model: if the base voltage is high enough (≈ 0.7 V idea), it switches on.
  • A divider + sensor can create the base voltage that controls switching.
  • Outputs might be an LED, buzzer, or other device (still needs correct resistors!).
🔍 Fault Finding (what to do when it doesn’t work)
  • Golden rule: change one thing at a time, then test again.
  • Check the basics first: correct power rails, correct polarity (LED/diode), correct resistor present.
  • Look for stripboard issues: missing/wrong track cuts, solder bridges, untrimmed legs shorting tracks.
  • Use measurements: check supply voltage, then check divider output voltage, then check the switching point.
🧰 Practical Skills (stripboard & soldering)
  • Stripboard tracks run in lines — you must add track cuts to stop unwanted connections.
  • Common tools: track cutter, solder sucker, heat sink (to protect components).
  • Good solder joint: shiny, secure, not a blob, not a dry/dull joint.
  • Good practice: keep iron tip clean and tinned, heat the joint (not the solder), keep parts still while cooling.
🔧 Components & What They Do
  • Voltage rails: +V and 0V lines that power the whole circuit.
  • Resistor: limits current / shares voltage.
  • Variable resistor: resistance can be adjusted.
  • SPDT switch: connects the middle contact to one of two outputs (select A or B).
  • Diode: allows current in one direction only.
  • LED: a diode that gives out light — needs a series resistor.
  • LDR: more light → lower resistance.
  • Thermistor (NTC): higher temperature → lower resistance.
  • Transistor (NPN): can act as an electronic switch.

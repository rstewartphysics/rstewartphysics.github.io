Core

13 marks

The demand of the real test — one rearrangement, one conversion or one intermediate step.

64 marks

A resistor is taken from the school stock box. Its colour bands are shown below.

(a) State the resistance of this resistor, in kΩ.1

(b) Calculate the maximum resistance this resistor could have and still be within its stated tolerance.2

(c) The resistor is measured with an ohmmeter, which reads 1·84 kΩ. State whether the resistor is within its stated tolerance. You must justify your answer.1

76 marks

A pupil sets up the circuit shown to test two resistors from the stock box.

(a) Calculate the total resistance of the circuit.1

(b) Calculate the reading on the ammeter. Give your answer in milliamperes.3

(c) Calculate the potential difference across the 4·7 kΩ resistor.2

83 marks

A pupil investigates a resistor R using an ammeter and a voltmeter, and plots the results shown.

(a) Use the gradient of the graph to determine the resistance of resistor R.2

(b) The pupil replaces resistor R with a filament lamp and repeats the experiment, obtaining the sketch graph shown. State what this graph shows about the resistance of the filament lamp as the current in it increases.1

Push

7 marks

Two or more steps, an unfamiliar context, and a judgement to justify.

95 marks

A gardener builds a soil-warming system for a greenhouse, powered from a 12 V supply. Two heating cables, each of resistance 47 Ω, are connected in parallel. A 15 Ω resistor protects the supply. Switch S brings the second cable into the circuit.

(a) Determine the total resistance of the circuit when switch S is closed.3

(b) Determine the reading on the ammeter when switch S is closed.2

102 marks

The gardener now connects a third identical 47 Ω heating cable in parallel with the other two. A friend says:

"Adding another cable adds more resistance to the circuit, so the reading on the ammeter will fall."
State whether the friend is correct, and justify your answer using the physics of parallel circuits.

S3 Physics – Electricity 3: Power and Concepts

Overview

This page is a clean revision hub for Electricity 3. It bridges S3 and National 5 by focusing on the key ideas that usually come up in end-of-block questions: charge and current, power and energy, choosing the correct power equation, fuses, AC and DC, oscilloscope traces, and electric fields.

Best revision order Read the KO first, then do booklet questions, then use the worked examples and quick practice here, and finish with mixed questions from the Past Paper Finder.
What usually gains marks Pick the correct equation, convert units first, substitute carefully, and always finish with a final answer and unit.
Big idea Electricity questions often look different, but the method is usually the same: identify what you know, choose the matching relationship, and work in standard units.
Revision checklisttap to open
  • I can use Q = It and its rearrangements.
  • I can use P = E / t and E = Pt.
  • I can choose between P = IV, P = I²R and P = V² / R.
  • I can use I = P / V for mains appliances and choose a fuse.
  • I can tell the difference between AC and DC.
  • I can identify AC and DC traces on an oscilloscope.
  • I can draw electric fields with arrows going from + to −.
  • I can predict the direction of a positive or negative charged particle in a field.

Equations and revision rules

Keep the method calm and consistent: convert first, choose the matching equation, then substitute with units.

Quantity Symbol Meaning Unit
Charge Q Amount of charge transferred coulomb (C)
Current I Rate of flow of charge ampere (A)
Power P Energy transferred each second watt (W)
Energy E Amount of energy transferred joule (J)
Time t How long the process lasts second (s)
Voltage V The push that drives charge volt (V)
Resistance R How much a component opposes current ohm (Ω)
Charge and current
Q = It
Use when you know current and time, or when you need to find charge, current, or time.
Power and energy
P = E / t
Rearrangement: E = Pt. Use when the question gives energy and time, or power and time.
Power from voltage and current
P = IV
Use this when the question gives I and V.
Power from current and resistance
P = I²R
Use this when the question gives I and R. Convert mA to A first.
Power from voltage and resistance
P = V² / R
Use this when the question gives V and R. Square the voltage first.
Fuse calculation
I = P / V
On UK mains, use V ≈ 230 V. Then choose a suitable fuse.
Quick unit conversionstap to open
minutes → seconds: multiply by 60
mA → A: divide by 1000
A → mA: multiply by 1000
kW → W: multiply by 1000
Which power equation should I use?tap to open
Know V and I? Use P = IV.
Know I and R? Use P = I²R.
Know V and R? Use P = V² / R.
Calculation method: circle the givens → convert units if needed → choose the matching equation → substitute with units → give a final answer with a unit.

Worked examples

Each example is hidden to keep the page tidy. Open only the one you need.

Worked example 1 — Q = Itcharge

Question: A current of 2.5 A flows for 6 minutes. Calculate the charge transferred.

Step 1: Convert time to seconds: 6 × 60 = 360 s

Step 2: Use Q = It

Step 3: Q = 2.5 × 360 = 900

Answer: 900 C

Worked example 2 — P = E / tpower

Question: A heater transfers 1800 J of energy in 45 s. Calculate the power.

Step 1: Use P = E / t

Step 2: P = 1800 / 45

Step 3: P = 40

Answer: 40 W

Worked example 3 — P = IVpower

Question: A component has a voltage of 12 V and a current of 0.35 A. Calculate the power.

Step 1: Use P = IV

Step 2: P = 12 × 0.35

Step 3: P = 4.2

Answer: 4.2 W

Worked example 4 — P = I²Rpower

Question: A resistor carries a current of 250 mA and has a resistance of 120 Ω. Calculate the power.

Step 1: Convert current: 250 mA = 0.25 A

Step 2: Use P = I²R

Step 3: P = (0.25)² × 120

Step 4: P = 0.0625 × 120 = 7.5

Answer: 7.5 W

Worked example 5 — P = V² / Rpower

Question: A 100 Ω resistor is connected to a 9.0 V supply. Calculate the power.

Step 1: Use P = V² / R

Step 2: P = 9.0² / 100

Step 3: P = 81 / 100 = 0.81

Answer: 0.81 W

Worked example 6 — Fuse choice using I = P / Vfuses

Question: A kettle is rated at 1840 W and uses UK mains. Calculate the current and choose a fuse.

Step 1: Use V = 230 V

Step 2: Use I = P / V

Step 3: I = 1840 / 230 = 8.0 A

Step 4: 3 A is too small, so choose the next suitable standard fuse.

Answer: Current = 8.0 A, so choose a 13 A fuse.

Worked example 7 — Charged particles in a fieldfields

Question: Two parallel plates are set up with the left plate positive and the right plate negative. Which way does a positive particle move? Which way does a negative particle move?

Step 1: Electric field arrows go from + to −, so the field points left to right.

Step 2: A positive particle moves in the same direction as the arrows.

Step 3: A negative particle moves in the opposite direction to the arrows.

Answer: Positive particle moves right. Negative particle moves left.

Quick practice

These are short mixed revision sets. Try them first, then open the self-check answers underneath each section.

Set 1 — Charge and currentQ = It
1. A current of 3 A flows for 8 s. Calculate the charge transferred.
2. 48 C of charge passes through a lamp in 12 s. Calculate the current.
3. A device carries a current of 0.4 A. How long does it take for 120 C of charge to flow?
4. Explain in one sentence what 1 A means.
Self-check answersanswers

1. 24 C

2. 4 A

3. 300 s

4. 1 coulomb of charge flows each second.

Set 2 — Power and energyP = E / t
1. A 60 W lamp is on for 20 s. Calculate the energy transferred.
2. 3600 J of energy is transferred in 60 s. Calculate the power.
3. A 100 W heater works for 5 minutes. Calculate the energy transferred. Hint: convert the time first.
4. Why does a higher power appliance transfer more energy each second?
Self-check answersanswers

1. 1200 J

2. 60 W

3. 5 min = 300 s, so E = Pt = 100 × 300 = 30 000 J

4. Because power is the amount of energy transferred each second.

Set 3 — Choosing the correct power equationmixed
1. A component has V = 6.0 V and I = 0.5 A. Calculate the power.
2. A resistor has I = 2.0 A and R = 5.0 Ω. Calculate the power.
3. A resistor has V = 12 V and R = 24 Ω. Calculate the power.
4. A resistor has I = 300 mA and R = 40 Ω. Calculate the power. Hint: convert mA to A first.
5. State which equation you would choose if the question gives V and R.
Self-check answersanswers

1. 3.0 W

2. 20 W

3. P = V² / R = 12² / 24 = 144 / 24 = 6 W

4. 300 mA = 0.3 A, so P = I²R = 0.3² × 40 = 0.09 × 40 = 3.6 W

5. P = V² / R

Set 4 — Fuses, AC/DC and oscilloscope tracesconcepts
1. A mains appliance is rated at 460 W. Calculate the current taken from a 230 V supply.
2. Which fuse should be used for the 460 W appliance: 3 A or 13 A?
3. State one difference between AC and DC.
4. A straight horizontal oscilloscope trace shows what type of supply?
5. A wave going above and below the centre line shows what type of supply?
Self-check answersanswers

1. I = P / V = 460 / 230 = 2 A

2. 3 A fuse

3. AC changes direction; DC goes in one direction only.

4. DC

5. AC

Set 5 — Electric fields and charged particlesfields
1. In an electric field, field lines go from which type of charge to which type of charge?
2. What do the arrows on field lines show?
3. Why must field lines never cross?
4. A positive particle is placed in a field. Does it move with the arrows or against them?
5. A negative particle is placed in a field. Does it move with the arrows or against them?
Self-check answersanswers

1. From positive to negative

2. The direction of force on a positive test charge

3. Because the field cannot point in two directions at the same place

4. With the arrows

5. Against the arrows

Common mistakes

These are the small errors that often cost marks even when the method is mostly correct.

Time not in seconds Convert minutes to seconds before using Q = It or P = E / t.
mA left as A Divide by 1000 before using power equations, especially before squaring in I²R.
Wrong final unit Charge = C, current = A, energy = J, power = W.
Forgetting to square In P = I²R and P = V² / R, square the value first.
Wrong fuse choice Calculate the current first using I = P / V, then choose a suitable fuse.
Field arrows wrong way Electric field arrows always go from + to −.
Field lines crossing Field lines never cross. Keep the pattern tidy and consistent.
No unit in the answer Even with the right number, missing the unit can lose an easy mark.
Final check before moving on: Have I converted units? Chosen the matching equation? Substituted correctly? Written a final answer with a unit?

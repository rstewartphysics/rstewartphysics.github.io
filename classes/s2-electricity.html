⚡ Static electricitykey ideas
  • Electrons can move from one material to another when rubbed.
  • Gaining electrons → becomes negative. Losing electrons → becomes positive.
  • Like charges repel. Opposite charges attract.
Quick check: Can you explain why a balloon can stick to hair after rubbing?
🔧 Symbols & circuit drawingsskills
  • Use straight lines and right angles where possible.
  • Symbols must be clear (cell/battery, lamp, switch, motor, buzzer).
  • Label meters clearly if a question uses A1/A2/V1 etc.
Quick check: Can you draw one series circuit and one parallel circuit from memory?
➡️ Series circuitsrules
  • There is one path for the current.
  • Current is the same everywhere in the loop.
  • Voltage is shared across components: battery voltage = sum of voltages.
  • If one bulb breaks, all go out.
Quick check: If a lamp is added in series, does the current increase or decrease?
🌿 Parallel circuitsrules
  • There are branches (more than one path).
  • Voltage is the same across each branch (same as the battery).
  • Total current adds up: current in = sum of currents in branches.
  • If one bulb breaks, others stay on (like house wiring).
Quick check: Why does adding a branch increase the total current from the battery?
📏 Meters (ammeter / voltmeter)must know
  • Ammeter measures current (A) and is placed in series.
  • Voltmeter measures voltage (V) and is placed in parallel across a component.
  • In diagrams, look for the meter connected across something (that’s the voltmeter).
Quick check: If meter P is across the lamp and Q is in the loop, which is V and which is A?
🔋 Cells & batteries (energy)core
  • In a chemical cell: chemical energy → electrical energy.
  • A battery is two or more cells connected together.
  • Rechargeable battery while charging: electrical energy → chemical energy.
Quick check: What energy change happens when charging a phone battery?
🧪 Chemical cells & electrochemical serieschallenge
  • Electricity can be made using two different metals and a salt solution.
  • Electrons flow through the wires; ions move through the solution.
  • The electrochemical series is a list of metals by willingness to give up electrons.
  • The further apart the metals are in the series, the bigger the voltage.
Quick check: Which pair would likely give the bigger voltage: metals close together or far apart?
📊 Graphs & data (easy marks)skills
  • Average = add values ÷ number of values.
  • Bar chart: labelled axes + units + sensible scale + accurate bars.
  • Use most of the graph paper (don’t squash the bars into one corner).
Quick check: Can you spot a bad scale on a bar chart and fix it?

Key rules (the ones that score marks)

If you’re stuck in a question, write the rule first, then apply it.

➡️ Series circuits

  • Current is the same everywhere in the loop.
  • Voltage adds up: battery V = V across each component added together.
  • One break → whole circuit stops.

🌿 Parallel circuits

  • Voltage is the same across each branch (same as the battery).
  • Total current adds up: current from battery = sum of branch currents.
  • One branch breaks → others can still work.

📏 Meters

  • Ammeter measures current (A) and goes in series.
  • Voltmeter measures voltage (V) and goes in parallel.
  • Voltmeter across a component tells you the energy per charge for that component.

🔋 Cells & energy

  • Chemical cell: chemical → electrical energy.
  • Charging rechargeable battery: electrical → chemical energy.
  • Two or more cells connected together = a battery.
Why does current add in parallel?
Adding a branch adds another path where charge can flow. More charge moving per second means a bigger total current from the supply. (Think “branches add currents” rather than “splitting”.)
🧠 Definition remindersquick marks
  • Current = flow of charge (electrons) around a circuit.
  • Voltage = a measure of the energy that pushes electrons around a circuit.
  • Series = one route. Parallel = branches.

📌 Course overview (Higher Physics)

Higher Physics is split into two units (Our Dynamic Universe and Particles & Waves) plus the Assignment. Use the sections below for a quick “what’s in the course” refresher.

📚 What you’ll study (tap to open)
⚡ Electricity & Circuits
  • Electrical quantities: current, voltage, resistance, power (as needed), circuit symbols and measurement.
  • Sources & internal resistance: emf vs terminal p.d., lost volts, internal resistance graphs.
  • Practical circuits: designing/testing circuits safely, interpreting diagrams and layouts.
  • Capacitors: charge storage, charge/discharge, time constant ideas and graph interpretation.
  • AC & DC: mains vs battery supply, basic waveform ideas (where used in questions).
🪐 Our Dynamic Universe
  • Motion & projectiles: vectors, component motion, projectile paths, graphs.
  • Forces: Newton’s laws, free-body diagrams, resultant force and acceleration.
  • Momentum: impulse, collisions/explosions (conservation), interpreting scenarios.
  • Gravitation: field strength, orbital motion, satellite ideas, inverse-square law.
  • Relativity: time dilation/length contraction (concept + simple calculations).
  • Expanding Universe: redshift, Hubble-style ideas, evidence for expansion.
🌊 Waves
  • Refraction: wave speed change, bending, ray diagrams and qualitative explanations.
  • Interference & diffraction: path difference ideas, patterns, what affects fringes.
  • Spectra: emission/absorption lines, what spectra tell us about sources.
🧩 Particles
  • Motion/forces in fields: charged particles in electric/magnetic fields (direction + calculations).
  • Wave–particle duality: photons, photoelectric effect, evidence and key definitions.
  • Nuclear reactions: fission/fusion, energy changes, particle equations where used.
  • Standard Model: quarks/leptons/bosons, conservation ideas, interpreting particle diagrams.

Assessment skills that matter:
calculations (with units), explanations in words, interpreting graphs/data, and linking ideas across topics.

🗓️ Timeline (by term)

Aligned with your Success Criteria sheet and the 23–24 Higher timeline, then adjusted to match your current plan. Each term expands with the likely topic sequence and key milestones. Default: collapsed

Term 1 (Aug–Oct): Electricity + early Our Dynamic Universe
  • Electricity & circuits: quantities, components, internal resistance, practical circuits, capacitors, AC/DC foundations.
  • Early ODU: motion skills (vectors, graphs) that you need for projectiles/forces.
  • Skills focus: rearranging equations, clear working, units + significant figures.

Good checkpoint idea: a short Electricity calculation check (graphs + internal resistance + capacitors).

Term 2 (Oct–Dec): Our Dynamic Universe + 3-week pre-Christmas block
  • ODU main run: projectiles → forces → momentum → gravitation → relativity → expanding universe.
  • Problem-solving: multi-step questions, selecting equations, explaining reasoning.

Final 3 weeks before Christmas (1 week each):

  • Week 1 Inverse square law (gravitational/field strength style questions).
  • Week 2 Refraction (bending, speed changes, explanations/diagrams).
  • Week 3 Interference (patterns + conditions for clear fringes).
Term 3 (Jan–Mar): Assignment + quantum/particles after February
  • Early term: finish any remaining ODU content and start/complete Assignment work.
  • Waves consolidation: strengthen explanations for refraction/interference and link to exam-style questions.

After February (roughly 1 week each):

  • Week 1 Spectra (line spectra, what it tells you, exam interpretation).
  • Week 2 Photoelectric effect (threshold frequency, evidence for photons).
  • Week 3 Standard Model (particles + interactions, interpreting particle diagrams).
  • Week 4 Nuclear reactions (fission/fusion, energy changes, particle equations).

Tip: after each “1-week block”, do a short mixed set of past-paper questions (3–6 questions) while it’s fresh.

Term 4 (Apr–May/Jun): consolidation + exam technique
  • Mixed revision: rotate ODU ↔ Particles & Waves so nothing fades.
  • Past papers: timed sections, then full papers, then fix weak areas.
  • Exam technique: show working, correct units, sensible rounding, and clear written explanations.

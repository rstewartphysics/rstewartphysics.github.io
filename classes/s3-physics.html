⚡ Electricity 1 — Voltage, Current & Resistance

Use this tab to revise for the Electricity 1 (Circuits & Resistance) assessment. Work through the topics, watch the videos, then try the practice questions.

🗺️ What this assessment is about

You will be tested on:

  • Circuit symbols and simple circuit diagrams
  • Voltage, current and resistance — meanings, symbols and units
  • Series and parallel circuits (rules for current and voltage)
  • Measuring resistance and using ohmmeters
  • Using V = I R to solve problems
  • Combining resistors in series and in parallel
  • How resistance changes with temperature (thermistors / conductors)

The practice papers for Electricity 1 use the same topics and skills as your class test.

📌 Core facts & equations to know

Key definitions
  • Voltage (V) – energy supplied per coulomb of charge.
  • Current (I) – amount of charge flowing per second.
  • Resistance (R) – how much a component opposes current.
Essential relationships
  • V = I R
  • Series: currents same, voltages share.
  • Parallel: voltages same, currents share.

Make sure you can rearrange V = I R to find any one of V, I or R and use the correct units (V, A, Ω).

✅ Must-do practice before the assessment

From your Electricity 1 practice pack:

  • Practice Paper 1 – multiple choice on circuit components, series / parallel rules and temperature & resistance; short problems on total resistance, V–I graphs and thermistors.
  • Practice Paper 2 – more multiple choice plus worked questions on series circuits, ohmmeters, current division in parallel and using V = IR with graphs.
  • Challenge Papers 1 & 2 – harder mixed problems on series–parallel combinations, non-ohmic behaviour, sensors and power / energy in circuits.

Try one full practice paper in timed conditions (about 30 minutes), then check your answers and fix any topics you miss.

📝 Self-check: am I ready?

Tick these off before your assessment:

  • I can draw and recognise the main circuit symbols.
  • I can define voltage, current and resistance with correct units.
  • I know the current and voltage rules for series and parallel circuits.
  • I can use an ohmmeter correctly and explain why components must be disconnected.
  • I can use V = I R to calculate any one of V, I or R.
  • I can calculate total resistance for series, parallel and combinations.
  • I can describe how resistance changes with temperature and explain thermistor behaviour.

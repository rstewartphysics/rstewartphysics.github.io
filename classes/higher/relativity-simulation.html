The speed of light is the same for everyone

Every observer measures the same speed of light in a vacuum, c = 3.00 × 108 m s−1, however fast they are moving. Two results follow: a moving clock runs slow, and a moving object is shorter. Set a speed below and watch both happen.

Light clocks

Set a speed, then compare the two clocks. 0.995c is about the speed of the muons in the worked example below.

Predict how the ticks of the moving clock compare with the clock at rest beside you.

  • A light clock is a pulse of light bouncing between two mirrors. One tick is one trip up and back.
  • To you, the moving clock's light follows a longer, slanted light path, still at c, so each tick takes longer than a tick of the clock at rest.
  • You measure a moving clock running slow. This is time dilation.

    Rocket and ruler

    The same speed as the light clocks: change it in either place.

    At 0.60c a rocket is measured 0.80 times as long as at rest. Predict what happens to its height.

    • The rocket at rest has length l, measured by someone beside it.
    • To an observer it is moving past, it is shorter: length l′. This is length contraction. Its height does not change.
    • The same factor as time dilation, √1 − (v/c)2: multiply for lengths, divide for times.
    Factor √1 − (v/c)2

      Words to know

      Light clock
      Light bouncing between two mirrors. One trip up and back is one tick.
      Time dilation
      A moving clock is measured to run slow: t′ is longer than t.
      Length contraction
      A moving object is measured to be shorter along its direction of motion: l′ is shorter than l.
      Frame of reference
      The point of view an observer measures from, and moves with.

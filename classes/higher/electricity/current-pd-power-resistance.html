Current, p.d. & resistance

Current is the rate of flow of charge, Q = It. Potential difference is the energy given to each coulomb of charge. Resistance opposes current: V = IR.

  • Ohmic conductor: V–I graph is a straight line through the origin — resistance is constant.
  • Non-ohmic (e.g. a lamp filament): the line curves — resistance rises as it heats up.
  • Series: current is the same everywhere; p.d.s add up.
  • Parallel: p.d. is the same across each branch; currents add; total resistance is less than the smallest branch.

Potential dividers

Two dividers across one supply — the voltage "across the bridge"

Put two potential dividers across the same supply. The voltage "across the bridge" is the p.d. between the two midpoints: work out each midpoint voltage and subtract, V = VP − VQ. It reads zero when the two dividers share the same ratio (R₁/R₂ = R₃/R₄).

Exam-style questions

Try each one fully, the SQA way, before opening the mark scheme.

1. A kettle element of resistance 26 Ω is connected to the 230 V mains. (a) Calculate the current in the element. (b) Calculate the power.

Mark scheme
(a) V = IR → 230 = I × 26 → I = 8.8 A
(b) P = IV = 8.8 × 230 = 2040 W (≈ 2.0 kW) [or P = V²/R = 230²/26 = 2030 W]
Marks: relationship, substitution, answer with unit — each step shown.

2. A 1.5 kΩ and a 4.5 kΩ resistor form a potential divider across a 6.0 V supply. Calculate the p.d. across the 4.5 kΩ resistor.

Mark scheme
V = (R / (R₁+R₂)) × V_s = (4.5 / (1.5 + 4.5)) × 6.0
= (4.5 / 6.0) × 6.0 = 4.5 V

3. Two dividers are connected across a 9.0 V supply. The left divider is R₁ = 2.0 kΩ (top) and R₂ = 4.0 kΩ (bottom); the right divider is R₃ = 6.0 kΩ (top) and R₄ = 3.0 kΩ (bottom). Calculate the p.d. between the two midpoints.

Mark scheme
V_P = (4.0 / 6.0) × 9.0 = 6.0 V
V_Q = (3.0 / 9.0) × 9.0 = 3.0 V
V = V_P − V_Q = 6.0 − 3.0 = 3.0 V
The reading would be 0 V only if the two dividers had the same ratio.

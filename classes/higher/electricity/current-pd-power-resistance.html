Concept 1

Charge & current

Charge
Q = I t
charge = current × time
  • Current is the rate of flow of charge — how many coulombs pass each second.
  • Conventional current flows from + to − around the circuit.
  • In a series loop the current is the same everywhere. At a junction the currents add (charge is conserved).

Resistor
Variable resistor

Lamp

Voltmeter
Ammeter
LDR
Thermistor
Worked example: a current of 0.40 A flows for 30 s. Find the charge.
Q=I t
Q=0.40 × 30
Q=12 C
Practice 1

A charge of 90 C passes a point in 1.0 minute. Calculate the current.

Answer
Q = It → 90 = I × 60 → I = 1.5 A
Concept 2

Potential difference, Ohm's law & resistance

Ohm's law
V = I R
p.d. = current × resistance
  • Potential difference is the energy given to each coulomb of charge.
  • Resistance opposes current. Rearrange Ohm's law as needed: R = V ÷ I.
  • Ohmic conductor → V–I graph is a straight line through the origin (constant R).
  • Non-ohmic (e.g. a lamp) → the line curves; resistance rises as it heats up.
Worked example: a 12 V supply drives 0.25 A through a resistor. Find its resistance.
V=I R
12=0.25 × R
R=12 ÷ 0.25 = 48 Ω
Practice 2

A 6.0 Ω resistor carries a current of 2.0 A. Calculate the p.d. across it.

Answer
V = IR = 2.0 × 6.0 = 12 V
Concept 3

Resistors in series & parallel

Series
RT = R₁ + R₂ + …
resistances add up
Parallel
1RT = 1R₁ + 1R₂ + …
total is less than the smallest branch
  • Series: same current everywhere; the p.d.s add to the supply voltage.
  • Parallel: same p.d. across each branch; the branch currents add.
  • For parallel, remember to flip back at the end: you find 1/RT first, then invert.
Worked example: a 6.0 Ω and a 3.0 Ω resistor in parallel.
1/RT=1/6.0 + 1/3.0
1/RT=0.1667 + 0.3333 = 0.5000
RT=1 ÷ 0.5000 = 2.0 Ω

The total (2.0 Ω) is less than the smallest branch (3.0 Ω).

Two 12 Ω resistors are connected in parallel. Calculate the total resistance.

Concept 4

Electrical power & energy

Power
P = I V = I2R = V2R
pick the form from what you're given
Energy
E = P t
energy transferred = power × time
  • Power is the energy transferred each second (watts = joules per second).
  • All three power formulae give the same answer — choose the one matching your known values.
  • For heat or energy over time, follow up with E = Pt.
Example A (given I and V): a motor draws 2.5 A at 12 V.
P=I V
P=2.5 × 12
P=30 W
Example B (given V and R): a 230 V kettle element of 26 Ω.
P=V² ÷ R
P=230² ÷ 26
P=2030 W (≈ 2.0 kW)
Practice 4

A current of 0.50 A flows through an 8.0 Ω resistor. Calculate the power dissipated.

Answer
P = I²R = 0.50² × 8.0 = 0.25 × 8.0 = 2.0 W
Concept 5

Potential dividers

Divider ratio
V₁V₂ = R₁R₂
voltages share in the ratio of the resistances
Divider output
V₁ = R₁R₁ + R₂ × Vs
p.d. across one of two series resistors
  • Two resistors in series split the supply voltage in the ratio of their resistances.
  • The bigger resistor takes the bigger share of the voltage.
  • Sensor dividers: swap one resistor for an LDR (resistance falls as light rises) or a thermistor (resistance falls as temperature rises) so the output voltage changes with the surroundings.

Worked example: 3.0 kΩ (top) and 7.0 kΩ (bottom) across 12 V — find the p.d. across the 7.0 kΩ.
V₁=(R₂ ÷ (R₁ + R₂)) × Vs
V₁=(7.0 ÷ (3.0 + 7.0)) × 12
V₁=(7.0 ÷ 10.0) × 12 = 8.4 V
Practice 5

A 1.5 kΩ (top) and 4.5 kΩ (bottom) divider is across 6.0 V. Find the p.d. across the 4.5 kΩ.

Answer
V = (4.5 / (1.5 + 4.5)) × 6.0 = (4.5/6.0) × 6.0 = 4.5 V
Concept 6

Two dividers across one supply

Voltage "across the bridge"
V = VP − VQ
p.d. between the two midpoints
  • Put two potential dividers across the same supply. Each midpoint voltage is just a divider output.
  • The voltage "across the bridge" is the difference between the two midpoints: work out VP and VQ, then subtract.
  • The reading is zero when the two dividers have the same ratio (R₁/R₂ = R₃/R₄).
Worked example: 9.0 V supply; left 2.0 kΩ/4.0 kΩ, right 6.0 kΩ/3.0 kΩ. Find the p.d. between the midpoints.
VP=(4.0 ÷ (2.0 + 4.0)) × 9.0 = 6.0 V
VQ=(3.0 ÷ (6.0 + 3.0)) × 9.0 = 3.0 V
V=VP − VQ = 6.0 − 3.0 = 3.0 V

Practice 6

Two dividers across 10 V: left 5.0 kΩ/5.0 kΩ, right 2.0 kΩ/8.0 kΩ. Find the p.d. between the midpoints.

Answer
V_P = (5/10)×10 = 5.0 V V_Q = (8/10)×10 = 8.0 V V = V_P − V_Q = 5.0 − 8.0 = −3.0 V (Q is 3.0 V higher than P)

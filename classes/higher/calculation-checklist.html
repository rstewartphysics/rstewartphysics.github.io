Three lines, three marks

Most Higher calculations are marked the same way. There is one mark for each line of working:

Relationship: copied from the relationships sheet.
Substitute: the numbers put into it, in SI units.
Answer + unit: rounded to suit the data.

A correct answer on its own gets all three marks. A wrong answer on its own gets none. Write all three lines, and a slip costs you one mark, not three.

The checklist

  1. Write the relationship first, exactly as the relationships sheet gives it, with every symbol labelled. One relationship, never a triangle.

    Choosing a wrong relationship loses every mark in the calculation. Example 1

  2. Show every power in the substitution: write (1.1 × 1010)2, not 1.1 × 1010.

    If the square is missing in your substitution, only the relationship mark is given. Example 2

  3. Change prefixes as you substitute: 6.6 km s−1 goes in as 6.6 × 103 m s−1.

    A prefix left in the numbers is a wrong substitution, so only the relationship mark is given. Example 3

  4. Choose a direction as positive and keep every sign to the end. A ball thrown upwards has u positive and a = −9.8 m s−2.

    Making u and a both positive is a wrong substitution. Example 4

  5. Keep every digit until the last line. Round only the final answer.

    Rounding part-way can push your answer outside the accepted values, and the answer mark is lost. Example 5

  6. Finish with the answer and its unit, rounded to the data: one figure fewer, up to two more, than the least precise value.

    No unit, or too many figures, loses the answer mark. Example 6

  7. “Show that”: write every step. The relationship, the substitution, then the value the question gave you.

    Working that does not start with a relationship gets no marks. Example 7

  8. Use your earlier answer, even if you think it is wrong. Never leave the next part blank.

    A wrong answer used correctly in the next part can still get full marks there. Example 8

Higher traps the examiners keep reporting

  • Up a slope at constant speed, the forward force is the weight down the slope plus friction. 2024 Q2(a)(ii)
  • Tension in a coupling: use the mass and friction of the thing being pulled. T = ma on its own scores 0. 2024 Q2(c)
  • G, not g, in F = Gm1m2/r2. Take constants from the data sheet. 2024 Q4(a)(ii)
  • Copy subscripts as subscripts. Turning a subscript into a power makes the relationship wrong. 2024 report
  • Gradients: carry the axis’s × 10ⁿ into the gradient, and use two points on your line, not data points. 2023 Q14(b), Q14(c)
  • No long calculator strings or “…” before the answer: copying them causes slips. 2024 report

Past-paper examples

Each habit on a real question. The right-hand answer follows the marking instructions. The left-hand answer is a typical answer that loses marks, written from the mistakes examiners reported.

1. Write the relationship first

2024 Paper 2, Q2(a)(i) · 3 marks

A car pulls a caravan up a slope at a constant speed of 4.0 m s−1. The slope is at an angle of 9.6° to the horizontal. The car and passengers have a total mass of 1650 kg. The caravan has a mass of 1350 kg.

Determine the component of the total weight of the car, passengers, and caravan acting down the slope.

F component of the weight down the slope (N) · W the whole weight (N) · m mass (kg) · g 9.8 N kg−1 · θ angle of the slope

✗ A typical answer that loses marks

W = mg sin θW = (1650 + 1350) × 9.8 × sin 9.6W = 4900 N

0 marks out of 3

W stands for the whole weight, so “W = mg sin θ” is a wrong relationship. The marking instructions do not accept it, so the right number cannot earn the other two marks.

✓ Gets all the marks

F = mg sin θF = (1650 + 1350) × 9.8 × sin 9.6F = 4900 N

3 marks out of 3

A labelled component, such as Wparallel = mg sin θ, is also accepted.

2. Show every power in the substitution

2024 Paper 2, Q4(a)(ii) · 3 marks

The mass of Didymos is taken to be 5.3 × 1011 kg. The mass of the Earth is 6.0 × 1024 kg. The distance between Earth and Didymos was 1.1 × 1010 m.

Determine the gravitational force between Earth and Didymos.

F force (N) · G 6.67 × 10−11 m3 kg−1 s−2 (data sheet) · m1, m2 masses (kg) · r distance between them (m)

✗ A typical answer that loses marks

F = Gm1m2r2 F = 6.67 × 10−11 × 6.0 × 1024 × 5.3 × 10111.1 × 1010 F = 1.9 × 1016 N

1 mark out of 3

The square is in the relationship but not in the substitution. Examiners reported this “dropped” square in this question.

✓ Gets all the marks

F = Gm1m2r2 F = 6.67 × 10−11 × 6.0 × 1024 × 5.3 × 1011(1.1 × 1010)2 F = 1.8 × 106 N

3 marks out of 3

3. Change prefixes as you substitute

2024 Paper 2, Q4(b)(i) · 3 marks

DART had a mass of 570 kg and was travelling at 6.6 km s−1 when it collided head-on with Dimorphos.

Calculate the maximum kinetic energy transferred from DART to Dimorphos during the collision.

Ek kinetic energy (J) · m mass (kg) · v speed (m s−1) · kilo (k) = × 103

✗ A typical answer that loses marks

Ek = ½mv2Ek = 0.5 × 570 × 6.62Ek = 1.2 × 104 J

1 mark out of 3

6.6 is in kilometres per second. Put in as 6.6 m s−1, it is a wrong substitution.

✓ Gets all the marks

Ek = ½mv2Ek = 0.5 × 570 × (6.6 × 103)2Ek = 1.2 × 1010 J

3 marks out of 3

4. Choose a direction as positive

2024 Paper 2, Q1(a)(iii) · 4 marks

The ball is thrown at 11.0 m s−1 at 36.0° to the horizontal, released 1.60 m above the ground. Its vertical component of velocity is 6.47 m s−1. The ball was directly above student B 0.95 s after it was released. Student B has a maximum reach of 2.10 m.

Determine the height h between student B and the ball.

s vertical displacement (m) · u initial vertical velocity (m s−1) · a acceleration (m s−2) · t time (s)

✗ A typical answer that loses marks

s = ut + ½at2s = (6.47 × 0.95) + (0.5 × 9.8 × 0.952)s = 10.6 mh = 1.60 + 10.6 − 2.10 = 10.1 m

1 mark out of 4

u and a are both positive, so the substitution is wrong. Examiners reported this as the most common mistake in this question.

✓ Gets all the marks

s = ut + ½at2s = (6.47 × 0.95) + (0.5 × (−9.8) × 0.952)h = 1.60 + ((6.47 × 0.95) + (0.5 × (−9.8) × 0.952)) − 2.10h = 1.2 m

4 marks out of 4

5. Keep every digit until the last line

2024 Paper 2, Q1(b)(i) · 3 marks

The loudspeaker in the ball produces a sound of frequency 622 Hz. The ball travels towards student B at a constant velocity of 8.60 m s−1.

Calculate the frequency of the sound heard by student B as the ball approaches them.

fo frequency heard (Hz) · fs frequency of the source (Hz) · v speed of sound, 3.40 × 102 m s−1 (data sheet) · vs speed of the source (m s−1)

✗ A typical answer that loses marks

fo = fs × vv − vs fo = 622 × 340340 − 8.60 fo = 622 × 1.03 fo = 641 Hz

2 marks out of 3

Rounding 1.0259… to 1.03 part-way gives 641 Hz, which is not one of the accepted answers.

✓ Gets all the marks

fo = fs × vv − vs fo = 622 × 340340 − 8.60 fo = 638 Hz

3 marks out of 3

Accepted: 640, 638, 638.1 or 638.14 Hz.

6. Finish with the answer and its unit

2024 Paper 2, Q2(c) · 3 marks

On a straight, level road the car and caravan accelerate at 0.16 m s−2. The caravan has a mass of 1350 kg. The frictional force acting on the caravan is 1200 N.

Determine the tension in the coupling between the car and caravan.

F unbalanced force (N) · m mass of the caravan (kg) · a acceleration (m s−2)

✗ A typical answer that loses marks

F = maF = 1350 × 0.16Tension = (1350 × 0.16) + 1200Tension = 1416

2 marks out of 3

There is no unit, so the answer mark is lost.

✓ Gets all the marks

F = maF = 1350 × 0.16Tension = (1350 × 0.16) + 1200Tension = 1400 N

3 marks out of 3

0.16 has two significant figures, so 1400 N. 1000, 1420 and 1416 N are also accepted.

7. “Show that”: write every step

2024 Paper 2, Q2(b)(i) · 2 marks

The car and caravan, moving at 4.0 m s−1, now accelerate uniformly up the slope for 250 s to a velocity of 9.5 m s−1.

Show that the acceleration of the car and caravan is 0.022 m s−2.

v final velocity (m s−1) · u initial velocity (m s−1) · a acceleration (m s−2) · t time (s)

✗ A typical answer that loses marks

a = 9.5 − 4.0250a = 0.022 m s−2

0 marks out of 2

It starts with numbers, not a relationship from the sheet. In a “show that”, nothing else can earn a mark until the relationship is written.

✓ Gets all the marks

v = u + at9.5 = 4.0 + (a × 250)a = 0.022 m s−2

2 marks out of 2

8. Use your earlier answer

2024 Paper 2, Q2(a)(ii) · 1 mark

The total frictional force acting on the car and caravan is 1800 N.

Determine the forward force produced by the car.

Both answers below got part (a)(i) wrong: they used cos instead of sin and found 29 000 N for the weight down the slope.

✗ A typical answer that loses marks

(left blank)

0 marks out of 1

Unsure of (a)(i), the pupil skipped (a)(ii).

✓ Gets the mark, even after a wrong (a)(i)

Forward force = 1800 + 29 000Forward force = 30 800 N

1 mark out of 1

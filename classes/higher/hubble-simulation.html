Galaxies are not flying apart through space. The space between them is stretching. Stretch a chain of galaxies and watch how far each one moves away from a home galaxy.

    Hubble's graph and the age of the Universe

    In 1929 Edwin Hubble plotted the recessional velocity of galaxies against their distance from us. The graph is a straight line through the origin, just like your washers graph. This is the Hubble–Lemaître law. Older papers call it Hubble's law.

    Why Hubble's age is so short. The 2018 paper asks this (Section 2 Q5). Answers the marking instructions accept:

    • State why the value is different: the value for H0 is too large; or there is not enough data at large distances; or new, more accurate data is available.
    • Suggest a change to the graph: draw the correct line of best fit; or use a larger sample, all of the 1929 data; or use current data.

    Hubble's distances were later found to be too small. With today's distances the same galaxies sit much further out, so the line is much less steep. Today's value, H0 = 2.3 × 10−18 s−1, gives 13.8 × 109 years.

    Words to know

    Recessional velocity
    The velocity of a galaxy moving away from us.
    Hubble constant, H0
    The gradient of the graph of recessional velocity against distance. Unit: s−1.
    Hubble–Lemaître law
    The recessional velocity of a galaxy is directly proportional to its distance from us: v = H0d.
    Expanding Universe
    Space itself is stretching, so every galaxy sees the others moving away.

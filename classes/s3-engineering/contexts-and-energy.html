Lesson 1 · SC 1–3

Engineers and what they do

Engineers use science and maths to solve real problems. There are four main types, and a big project needs all four.

Lesson 1 slides PDF · 43 slides

The four main types of engineering

  • Electrical — electricity and circuits: power supplies, wiring, lighting and control systems.
  • Mechanical — machines and moving parts: engines, gears, pumps and brakes.
  • Civil — structures: roads, bridges, tunnels, dams and buildings.
  • Chemical — materials and chemical processes: fuels, plastics, medicines and paints.

One project, many engineers

The Edinburgh Trams needed every type of engineer.

  • Civil engineers designed the track bed, the bridges and the tram stops.
  • Electrical engineers designed the overhead lines that supply power to the trams.
  • Mechanical engineers worked on the trams' motors, brakes and doors.
  • Chemical engineers chose materials, such as the coating that stops the steel rusting.
Worked example · what + why

Describe one task a civil engineer would carry out on a flood scheme.

✗ “Builds walls.” — 0 marks. It doesn't say what the wall is for.

✓ “Designs the embankments that hold back flood water (what), so that they are strong enough to stop homes flooding (why).” — 1 mark.

Design, build, test, improve

  • Design — sketch ideas, plan, and choose materials.
  • Build — make a prototype: a first working version, made so it can be tested.
  • Test — try it, measure it and find any faults.
  • Improve — change the design to fix the faults, then test again.

Engineers go round this loop again and again until the product works. Your marble run went round it too.

Quick check

Choose an answer for each, then mark them. Get 3 or more right to complete this part.

1. Which type of engineer is most likely to design the gears in a bicycle?

Gears are moving parts of a machine, which is mechanical engineering.

2. A chemical engineer is most likely to…

Chemical engineers work with materials and chemical processes, such as making fuels.

3. A first working version of a product, made so it can be tested, is called a…

A prototype is built in the Build stage so it can be tested.

4. Which answer would get the mark for “Describe one task an electrical engineer carries out”?

It says what the task is and why it matters. The others are too vague.

Lesson 2 · SC 4–8

Impacts of engineering

An impact is an effect or change caused by something. Every project has three types of impact, and most have good and bad ones.

Lesson 2 slides PDF · 46 slides

Three types of impact

  • Social — people's lives: safety, travel, health, communication and services.
  • Economic — money and jobs: jobs, costs, profits, tourism and trade.
  • Environmental — the natural world: pollution, carbon emissions, habitats and waste.

Watch out: “it saves money” is economic, even when the project is a green one.

One project, all three types

The Borders Railway reopened in 2015.

  • Social: people in Galashiels can reach work, college or hospital in Edinburgh without a car.
  • Economic: tourists come by train and spend money in Borders shops.
  • Environmental: fewer car journeys, so less exhaust and less CO₂.

Both sides: what + why + who

A strong answer says what the effect is, why it happens, and who or what is affected. “One positive and one negative” means two different effects.

Worked example · smartphones, social

✗ “They're useful.” — 0 marks.

✓ Positive: people can call for help from anywhere, so they are safer when out alone.

✓ Negative: some people use their phones late at night, so they get less sleep.

Real examples from Scotland

Using a real, named project earns marks. Learn one effect for each of these.

Worked example · building up an answer

Describe one negative environmental effect of a hydro-electric dam, and how engineers reduced it.

0 marks: “Dams are bad for fish.”

What + why: “The dam blocks the river, so salmon can't swim upstream to breed.”

With a real example: “At Pitlochry Dam, engineers built a fish ladder so salmon can still get past.”

Quick check

Choose an answer for each, then mark them. Get 3 or more right to complete this part.

1. A new supermarket creates 80 jobs in a town. This is a…

Jobs are about money, so the effect is economic, and more jobs is positive.

2. A new road means an ambulance reaches a village faster. This is a…

It changes people's lives (safety and health), so it is social.

3. Which of these is an environmental effect of a new airport?

Carbon dioxide affects the natural world. The others are economic or social.

4. Which answer would get the mark for “Describe one negative effect of a new quarry on people living nearby”?

It says what the effect is, why it happens, and who is affected.

Lesson 3 · SC 9–12

Energy in, energy out

Energy is never used up. It is transferred from one form to another, and some always ends up where we don't want it.

Lesson 3 slides PDF · 40 slides

Eight forms of energy

Energy is measured in joules (J). Three forms are stores, and five are energy on the move.

  • Chemical — stored in fuel, food and batteries.
  • Gravitational potential — stored in anything raised up high.
  • Elastic — stored in anything squashed or stretched, like a spring.
  • Kinetic — anything moving.
  • Electrical, heat, light and sound.

Watch out: a battery stores chemical energy, not electrical. Always say “gravitational potential”, not just “potential”.

Input, useful and wasted energy

  • Input energy — the energy that goes in.
  • Useful output energy — the energy that does the job we want.
  • Wasted energy — energy that doesn't do the job, usually heat or sound. It spreads out into the surroundings.

Watch out: the input is what goes in. A wind turbine's input is the kinetic energy of the wind; electrical is its output.

Drawing an energy transfer diagram — 2 marks

  1. Draw a box and write the device in it.
  2. Draw the input arrow going in on the left.
  3. Draw the useful output arrows coming out on the right.
  4. Draw the wasted energy on its own arrow, going down.

Mark 1: the input and useful output are right. Mark 2: the wasted energy is shown separately. If heat is drawn as useful when it isn't, the second mark is lost.

Useful or wasted? It depends on the job

Quick check

Choose an answer for each, then mark them. Get 4 or more right to complete this part.

1. A lorry is parked at the top of a hill. Which form of energy does it have more of than at the bottom?

Anything raised up high stores gravitational potential energy.

2. What is the input energy of a gas cooker?

Gas is a fuel, and fuels store chemical energy. Heat is the useful output.

3. An electric toothbrush's useful output is kinetic energy. Which of these is wasted?

The buzzing sound doesn't clean your teeth, so it is wasted.

4. Why is wasted energy drawn on its own arrow?

Keeping it separate shows it isn't useful. That is the second mark.

5. What happens to wasted energy?

Energy is never used up; wasted energy spreads out and warms the surroundings.

Lesson 4 · SC 13–18

Efficiency, power and teams

Efficiency tells you how much of the input energy does the useful job. Power tells you how fast energy is transferred.

Lesson 4 slides PDF · 46 slides
Percentage efficiency
efficiency = useful output energyinput energy × 100
The data booklet writes η = Eout / Ein. Multiply by 100 to get a percentage.
Power
P = Et
P in watts (W), E in joules (J), t in seconds (s).

Calculating efficiency

Efficiency is always a percentage and can never be more than 100 %, because you can't get out more energy than you put in.

Worked example

A car engine takes in 400 J of chemical energy from petrol and gives out 140 J of kinetic energy. Calculate its percentage efficiency.

efficiency = 140 ÷ 400 × 100 (1 mark)

= 35 % (1 mark)

The other 260 J is wasted, mostly as heat.

Why engineers improve efficiency

A more efficient machine wastes less energy, so:

  • it costs less to run — lower energy bills;
  • less fuel is burned at power stations, so less CO₂ is released;
  • it runs cooler, so it lasts longer;
  • batteries last longer between charges.

Watch out: “to waste less energy” on its own scores 0. That is what efficiency means. Say why it matters.

Power: how fast energy is transferred

1 watt means 1 joule transferred every second.

Worked example

A microwave oven transfers 26 400 J of energy in 24 s. Calculate its power.

P = E ÷ t = 26 400 ÷ 24 (1 mark)

= 1100 W (1 mark)

Energy use, cost and the environment

Using more energy means a bigger bill. Most electricity still comes partly from burning gas, so it also means more CO₂, which adds to climate change.

Ways to cut both: switch things off, use sensors and timers, insulate buildings, and choose efficient appliances.

The marble run: where the energy goes

  1. At the top, the marble has gravitational potential energy.
  2. As it rolls down, gravitational potential changes into kinetic energy. Friction and air resistance transfer some energy to heat and sound, which is wasted.
  3. So it reaches the cup with less kinetic energy than it would have had without friction, and it is moving more slowly.

Worked example: a bouncing ball

Why doesn't a dropped ball bounce back up to the height it started from?

Each bounce transfers some energy to heat and sound (what). So less kinetic energy is left, and the ball can't climb as high (why).

Good engineering teamwork

A good team:

  • shares out the jobs, so everyone has one;
  • listens to every idea, so the best one is picked;
  • agrees a sketch first, so everyone builds the same design;
  • tests together, so faults are found early.

Watch out: “we worked together” scores 0. Say what the team did and why it helped.

Quick check

Choose an answer for each, then mark them. Get 4 or more right to complete this part.

1. A lamp takes in 800 J of electrical energy and gives out 200 J of light. What is its percentage efficiency?

200 ÷ 800 × 100 = 25 %. The other 600 J is wasted as heat.

2. A games console transfers 9000 J of energy in 45 s. What is its power?

P = E ÷ t = 9000 ÷ 45 = 200 W.

3. What is the unit of power?

Power is measured in watts (W): joules per second.

4. Why can efficiency never be more than 100 %?

The useful output is always part of the input, never more than it.

5. Which answer would get the mark for “Describe one thing a good team did”?

It says what the team did and why it helped.

Concept 1

Resistor values: colour code & BS1852

  • Colour code (three colour bands + a tolerance band): bands 1 & 2 are digits, band 3 is the multiplier, and the tolerance band gives the ± range.
  • BS1852 / R-notation: the letter (R, K or M) does two jobs. It gives the size — R = ohms, K = ×1000, M = ×1 000 000 — and it marks where the decimal point goes. Examples: 270R = 270 Ω, 5K8 = 5.8 kΩ, 2M7 = 2.7 MΩ.
  • Tolerance: the real resistor can be a little above or below its label. Tolerance tells you the highest and lowest it could be.

Resistor decoder

Set the four bands. The value, the BS1852 code and the tolerance range update live.

Practice 1

Write 4 700 Ω and 5 800 Ω in BS1852 notation, and give the resistance band sequence for 4.7 kΩ (yellow, violet, red).

Answer
4 700 Ω = 4K7; 5 800 Ω = 5K8. 4.7 kΩ = yellow (4), violet (7), red (×100).

Practice 2

State which board you would use to test an idea you expect to change several times, and why.

Answer
A prototype board (breadboard) — components push in and out without soldering, so the circuit is quick and easy to change.
Concept 3

Soldering safely & non-permanent methods

⚠️
Soldering safety — the iron reaches over 350 °C A hot iron, molten solder and the fumes it gives off can all burn or harm you. Always:
  • Return the iron to its stand the moment you are not using it.
  • Solder in a well-ventilated area or use fume extraction — never breathe the fumes.
  • Hold work in a clamp or stand, not your fingers, and let joints cool before touching.
  • Wear eye protection and wash your hands afterwards.

Permanent joints are made by soldering; non-permanent joints use prototype boards, screw terminals or plug/socket connectors.

Exam tip: "construction using safe working practices" is worth most of the practical marks — be ready to state at least two specific soldering safety measures.
Practice 3

State two safety measures you must follow when soldering a circuit.

Answer
Any two, e.g.: keep the iron in its stand when not in use; solder in a well-ventilated area / use fume extraction; clamp the work rather than holding it; wear eye protection; let joints cool before touching.

Wiring conventions on a build

Follow these conventions so a board is neat, reliable and easy to fault-find:

ConventionWhy / where
Single-core wirefor Wye links and permanent connections on the board — holds its shape and lies flat.
Multicore (stranded) wirefor flying leads — connections that flex or move (to switches, off-board parts) without the core snapping.
Colour codingidentify each signal at a glance: red = +, black = 0 V, and a distinct colour for each other signal.
Joining separate boardsuse crimped connectors or terminal blocks so boards can be linked and unplugged cleanly.
Spiral wrap & cable tiesgather wire bundles together and keep them tidy, so wires don't snag or pull on their joints.
  • Securing & looming: bundle wires together and hold them in place with cable ties, a spiral wrap, heat-shrink, cable lacing / a loom, or P-clips — so wires don't pull on their joints, snag or get trapped.
  • Advantage of colour-coding or numbering: each wire is easy to identify, so it is quicker to connect correctly and to fault-find.
  • Examples of where it's used: mains plug wiring (brown = live, blue = neutral, green-yellow = earth); the red stripe on ribbon cable marking pin 1; resistor colour bands; and numbered terminal blocks / network cables.
Practice 4

(a) A signal must travel a long way without picking up electrical interference — state a suitable cable. (b) State one valid way to secure a bundle of wires. (c) Give one advantage of colour-coding wiring and one example of where it is used.

Answer
(a) Co-axial (screened) or fibre-optic cable. (b) A cable tie / spiral wrap / heat-shrink / cable lacing (loom) / P-clip. (c) Advantage: you can identify each wire quickly, so it's easier to connect correctly and trace faults; example: mains plug wiring (brown/blue/green-yellow), the red stripe on ribbon cable, or numbered terminal blocks.
Concept 5

Circuit ↔ layout diagrams & missing components

  • A circuit diagram shows components as symbols and how they connect electrically.
  • A layout diagram shows where each part physically sits on the board and how the tracks/wires run.
  • You must be able to convert between the two, and complete a circuit diagram from a specification by adding any missing components (using the symbol reference).
Practice 5

A specification says an LED must be protected from too much current. Its circuit diagram shows only the LED and supply. State the missing component and where it goes.

Answer
A series resistor (current-limiting resistor) in series with the LED, between the LED and the supply.

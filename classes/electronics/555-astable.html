How the 555 Astable Works

This section explains the core idea behind the 555 astable circuit. Important misconception: The capacitor does NOT fully charge or fully discharge. It only moves between 1/3 and 2/3 of the supply voltage.

📘 Click to expand explanation

The capacitor charges through R1 and R2 until it reaches 2/3 of the supply voltage. The 555 then switches LOW and the capacitor discharges through only R2 until it drops below 1/3. This loop repeats forever, creating a continuous square wave.

🔍 Spot the Difference: Schematic vs Stripboard

🟦 1. Shape Differences

Schematics are neat. Stripboards follow physical copper strips, so things move around.

🟥 2. IC Orientation

The 555 sits across the centre gap on stripboard.

🟧 3. Resistors Move

Their positions shift, but connections stay the same.

🟩 4. Capacitor Position

C1 is lower down but connects to pins 2/6 and 0V.

🟪 5. LED Positions

LEDs are moved sideways, still linked to pin 3.

🟫 6. Power Rails

Long coloured copper strips instead of symbolic rails.

🟨 7. Wires Appear

Stripboard drawings show link wires the schematic does not.

Key idea: The stripboard layout looks different, but every electrical connection is identical.

🎓 Student-Friendly Explanation

This circuit makes two LEDs flash one after the other using a 555 timer.

🌟 How it works

The capacitor charges and discharges repeatedly. R1 and R2 control how quickly this happens. The 555 output switches HIGH/LOW based on the capacitor voltage.

HIGH → green LED on
LOW → red LED on

The 680Ω resistors limit current and stop LEDs burning out.

🛠️ Stripboard Build Guide

Step 1 — Identify the parts

555 IC, R1, R2, C1, LEDs, LED resistors, power rails.

Step 2 — Place the 555

Sits across the centre gap.

Step 3 — Power rails

Red = +9V, Black = 0V.

Step 4 — R1 and R2

R1: pin 8 → pin 7
R2: pin 7 → pins 6/2

Step 5 — Capacitor

C1 connects between pins 2/6 and 0V.

Step 6 — LEDs

Green = HIGH, red = LOW.

Step 7 — Link wires

Used wherever copper strips cannot make the connection.

Important: Stripboard looks different but is electrically identical to the schematic.

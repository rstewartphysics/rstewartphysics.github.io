How the 555 Astable Works

This section explains the core idea behind the 555 astable circuit. Important misconception: The capacitor does NOT fully charge or fully discharge. It only moves between 1/3 and 2/3 of the supply voltage.

📘 Click to expand explanation

The capacitor charges through R1 and R2 until it reaches 2/3 of the supply voltage. The 555 then switches LOW and the capacitor discharges through only R2 until it drops below 1/3. This loop repeats forever, creating a continuous square wave.

Circuit Diagram & Stripboard Layout

This picture shows the schematic (right) and the equivalent stripboard layout (left). They look different, but every electrical connection is identical.

🔍 Spot the Difference: Schematic vs Stripboard

🟦 1. Shape Differences

The schematic shows a neat, logical layout. The stripboard follows the copper strips, so parts shift sideways or downwards to fit the board.

🟥 2. 555 Chip Placement

The stripboard has continuous copper strips (no centre gap). This means track cuts must be made under specific pins so they don’t join together. These cuts are shown as the red X markings under the IC.

Important: The chip still keeps the same pin numbering (pin 1 at the notch). The physical position changes, but the electrical connections do not.

🟧 3. R1 and R2 Positions

On the schematic they are vertical. On the stripboard they are placed horizontally and further apart — but still connect:

  • R1: pin 8 → pin 7
  • R2: pin 7 → pins 6/2

🟩 4. Capacitor Position (C1)

C1 sits far lower on the stripboard, but still connects the same node:

  • Top leg → linked pins 2 & 6
  • Bottom leg → 0 V rail

The long yellow link wire brings the 2/6 node down to the capacitor.

🟪 5. LED Positions

The LEDs are moved to the right-hand side of the board. They still connect correctly:

  • Green LED: pin 3 → resistor → 0 V
  • Red LED: +9 V → resistor → pin 3

The vertical yellow and black link wires create these paths.

🟫 6. Power Rails

Unlike the schematic’s symbolic rails, the stripboard uses long copper strips:

  • Top red strip: +9 V
  • Bottom black strip: 0 V

🟨 7. Link Wires

The schematic hides many connections because wires aren’t shown unless needed. On stripboard, link wires are required to join separate strips together. In the photo:

  • Yellow links join the timing nodes
  • Blue link joins pin 7 to R2
  • Black/red links extend power rails down the board

Key idea: The stripboard layout looks different, but every electrical connection is identical to the schematic. Follow the pin numbers, not the physical positions.

🎓 Student-Friendly Explanation

This circuit uses a 555 timer to make two LEDs flash one after the other. The 555 is running in astable mode, which means it never stays in one state — it keeps switching continuously.

🌟 What the circuit is doing

1. The capacitor keeps charging and discharging.
Its voltage rises up to about ⅔ of the supply and falls down to about ⅓ of the supply. It never charges all the way to 9 V — this is a common misconception.

2. R1 and R2 control how long each part of the cycle lasts.
• Bigger resistors = slower cycle (longer flashes)
• Smaller resistors = faster cycle (shorter flashes)

3. The 555 output switches HIGH and LOW based on capacitor voltage.
• HIGH → the green LED lights (current flows from pin 3 down to 0 V).
• LOW → the red LED lights (current flows from +9 V down to pin 3).

4. The two 680 Ω resistors protect the LEDs by limiting current so the LEDs do not burn out.

🔁 Summary of the repeating cycle

Charge → Output HIGH (green on) → Discharge → Output LOW (red on) → and the cycle repeats forever.

🛠️ Stripboard Build Guide

Step 1 — Identify all parts

Before placing anything, make sure you can point to each part on BOTH the schematic and the stripboard photo:

  • 555 IC (8-pin)
  • R1 and R2 (timing resistors)
  • C1 (timing capacitor, electrolytic)
  • Green LED + 680 Ω resistor (output HIGH)
  • Red LED + 680 Ω resistor (output LOW)
  • +9 V rail (red line)
  • 0 V rail (black line)
  • Track cuts under pins (shown as red X symbols)

Important: The stripboard layout looks different, but the connections must match the schematic exactly.

Step 2 — Place the 555 (with correct track cuts)

The stripboard in the photo uses continuous copper strips with no centre gap. Therefore: track cuts must be made under the pins instead of using a centre gap.

  • Cut the copper tracks under each pin shown with a red X
  • The notch or dot marks pin 1 (bottom-left in the photo)
  • Pins 1–4 run down one side, pins 5–8 run down the other
  • Each pin must be isolated on its own section of track

Step 3 — Set up the power rails

  • Red strip = +9 V
  • Black strip = 0 V (ground)
  • Both rails run horizontally across the whole board

Check with a continuity tester before connecting parts.

Step 4 — Fit the timing resistors R1 and R2

They must match the schematic pin connections:

  • R1: pin 8 → pin 7
  • R2: pin 7 → pins 6/2

Pins 2 and 6 must be linked — they form the timing node.

Step 5 — Add C1 (electrolytic capacitor)

C1 connects between:

  • Linked pins 2/6 (positive leg)
  • 0 V rail (negative leg)

Common error: Reversing the capacitor or connecting it to +9 V.

Step 6 — Add the LEDs + their 680 Ω resistors

Green LED (ON when output is HIGH)

  • Pin 3 → 680 Ω → 0 V

Red LED (ON when output is LOW)

  • +9 V → 680 Ω → pin 3

This opposite direction is why one lights on HIGH and the other on LOW.

Step 7 — Add the link wires

Add short wires wherever the schematic shows a connection but the stripboard does not naturally join those points:

  • Yellow links = timing node connections
  • Blue links = LED paths
  • Red / black = extensions of the power rails

Final Reminder

The layout in the photo looks different from the schematic, but every electrical connection must match exactly.
If something doesn’t work: check the track cuts, check pin numbers, then check LED polarity.

Concept 1

What an astable does

Key words

Astable
a circuit with no stable state. Its output switches high and low by itself, again and again.
Mark time
how long the output stays high in each cycle.
Space time
how long the output stays low in each cycle.
Period
one mark plus one space. The time for one cycle.
Frequency
how many cycles each second, in hertz (Hz).

An astable never settles. Nobody presses a switch, but the output on pin 3 keeps changing.

  • Pin 3 goes high. LED1, the red LED, comes on. This is the mark time.
  • Pin 3 goes low. LED2, the green LED, comes on. This is the space time.
  • Then it starts again. One mark plus one space is one period.

The frequency is how many periods fit in one second. Our board flashes about 20 times in 30 s.

You meet astables in flashing warning lights, bike lights and the clock that runs a counter.

Practice 1

An astable's output is high for 0.3 s, then low for 0.2 s, again and again.

(a) State the mark time. (b) State the period.

Answer
(a) 0.3 s, the time it is high. (b) 0.5 s: one mark plus one space, 0.3 + 0.2.

Concept 2

The 555 timer's pins

Key words

Threshold
pin 6. When C1 reaches ⅔ of the supply, pin 3 goes low.
Trigger
pin 2. When C1 falls to ⅓ of the supply, pin 3 goes high.
Discharge
pin 7. It empties C1 while pin 3 is low.

It is the same chip as in Lesson 3. Hold it with the notch at the top. Pin 1 is top left.

  • Pins 8 and 4 go to +5 V. Pin 1 goes to 0 V.
  • Pins 2 and 6 are joined. Both watch the voltage across C1.
  • Pin 7 sits between R1 and R2. It empties C1.
  • Pin 3, output, drives both LEDs.
  • Pin 5 is not used.
Practice 2

State the pin number for each job.

  • (a) empties C1
  • (b) is joined to pin 6
  • (c) drives the two LEDs
Answer
(a) Pin 7, discharge. (b) Pin 2, trigger. (c) Pin 3, output.

Concept 3

Read the circuit diagram

Key words

Timing parts
R1, R2 and C1. They set how fast it flashes.
Electrolytic capacitor
C1. It has a + leg, so it must go the right way round.
Test point
a pin on the board where you put the meter or the probe.

This is the circuit in your booklet.

  • R1 runs from +5 V to pin 7. R2 runs from pin 7 to pins 6 and 2.
  • C1 runs from pins 6 and 2 to 0 V, + side up.
  • R3 and LED1, red, go from pin 3 down to 0 V.
  • R4 and LED2, green, come from +5 V down to pin 3.

R3 and R4 limit the current. Each LED carries about 11 mA: (5 V − 2 V) ÷ 270 Ω.

Test points T1 to T4 are where you put the meter and the probe.

Another way to draw it

Most exam papers draw the astable with one LED. R3 and LED1 go from pin 3 to 0 V. It flashes at the same rate.

The timing part of the circuit, R1, R2 and C1, stays the same.

Practice 3

State the job of R3 and R4.

Answer
They limit the current through LED1 and LED2, so the LEDs are not damaged.

Concept 4

How C1 makes pin 3 switch

Key words

Charge
C1 fills through R1 and R2. The voltage across it rises.
Discharge
C1 empties through R2 into pin 7. The voltage across it falls.
⅓ and ⅔ of the supply
1.7 V and 3.3 V on a 5 V supply. C1 swings between them.

Follow one cycle:

  1. Pin 3 is high. LED1 is on.
  2. C1 charges through R1 and R2. The voltage across C1 rises.
  3. At ⅔ of the supply, pin 3 goes low. LED2 comes on.
  4. Pin 7 connects to 0 V inside the chip. C1 discharges through R2 only.
  5. At ⅓ of the supply, pin 3 goes high again. The cycle repeats.

C1 stays between ⅓ and ⅔ of the supply. It swings from 1.7 V to 3.3 V and back.

Watch out: R1 is only in the charging path. A bigger R1 makes the mark time longer. It does not change the space time.

Practice 4

Describe how the astable keeps switching. (3)

Answer
  • While pin 3 is high, C1 charges through R1 and R2 (1).
  • At ⅔ of the supply, pin 3 goes low and C1 discharges through R2 into pin 7 (1).
  • At ⅓ of the supply, pin 3 goes high again and the cycle repeats (1).

You can now…

  • describe one cycle, one step for each mark.
  • say which resistors C1 charges and discharges through.

    Concept 6

    Simulate it in Yenka

    Key words

    Voltmeter
    measures the voltage across C1. Connect it from C1's + leg to 0 V.
    Logic indicator
    shows the logic level. In Yenka, red = high and white = low.

    This is booklet Task 1. Build the circuit in Yenka first, then add:

    • a voltmeter across C1
    • a logic indicator on pin 3

    Count how many times the logic indicator turns red in 30 seconds. Then divide by 30 to get the frequency.

    Watch the voltmeter. Record C1's lowest and highest voltage.

    Practice 6

    In Yenka, the voltmeter across C1 has just reached 3.3 V.

    State which LED comes on next. Give a reason.

    Answer
    LED2, the green LED. 3.3 V is ⅔ of the supply, so pin 3 goes low. The logic indicator turns white.

    Concept 7

    Build it on stripboard

    This is booklet Tasks 2 to 4. The guide takes you from the diagram to the stripboard, one net at a time. A net is a set of legs that are joined. On the parts steps it is your turn: tap where each leg goes before the answer shows. Then find the mistake on three faulty boards.

    Pre-power-up check

    Do this before you connect the supply. Tick each one in booklet Task 4.

    1. No beep between T1 and T2 on continuity: no short across the supply.
    2. No solder bridges between tracks. The track cuts under IC1 are clean: continuity, no beep.
    3. IC1 in its socket the right way round: notch and pin 1 at the marked end.
    4. C1, LED1 and LED2 the right way round.
    5. Every link matches the wiring schedule.
    6. The supply is set to 5 V before it is connected.
    Practice 7

    State what a solder bridge is. Explain why it is a fault.

    Answer
    A blob of solder that joins two tracks. It joins two nets that should be apart, so parts are shorted out and the circuit will not work.

    Meter and probe on the astable

    The meter is on DCV 20 with the black probe on T2. The probe is clipped to +5 V and 0 V, on TTL and Pulse.

    Tool
    Test point

    Fault from readings

    Compare each reading with the one you expect. The first one that is wrong tells you where to look.

    • T1 not 5 V: look at the supply leads.
    • T4 does not rise and fall: look at R1, R2, C1 and the link from pin 2 to pin 6.
    • T3 does not flash: look at pins 4 and 8, and at IC1 itself.
    • T3 flashes but one LED stays dark: look at that LED and its resistor.
    Practice 8

    LED1 never lights. LED2 flashes. T1 reads 5.0 V. The probe on T3 flashes red and green.

    Suggest the fault.

    Answer
    The 555 is working: pin 3 is pulsing. The fault is in LED1's branch. LED1 is the wrong way round, or R3 or LED1 is not joined.

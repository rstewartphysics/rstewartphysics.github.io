📘 How the 555 Astable Works (click to expand)

The capacitor charges through R1 and R2 until it reaches 2/3 of the supply. The 555 then switches LOW and the capacitor discharges through R2 until it drops below 1/3. This repeats, creating a continuous square wave.

🔍 Spot the Difference: Schematic vs Stripboard (click to expand)

🟦 1. Shape Differences

Schematics are neat. Stripboards follow physical copper strips, so things move around.

🟥 2. IC Orientation

Schematics show tidy pin layout. Stripboard places the 555 across the centre gap.

🟧 3. Resistors Move

They may look far apart but still connect to the same pins on the 555.

🟩 4. Capacitor Position

C1 sits lower on stripboard but connects to exactly the same points.

🟪 5. LED Positions

The LEDs move sideways but still connect to pin 3.

🟫 6. Power Rails

Stripboard uses long coloured copper rails instead of symbolic +9V/0V dots.

🟨 7. Wires Appear

Stripboard drawings show coloured link wires not shown in schematics.

Key idea: The stripboard looks different, but every connection is electrically identical.

🎓 Student-Friendly Explanation (click to expand)

This circuit makes two LEDs flash one after the other using a 555 timer.

🌟 How it works

The capacitor charges and discharges repeatedly. R1 and R2 control how quickly this happens. The 555 output switches HIGH/LOW based on the capacitor level.

HIGH → green LED on LOW → red LED on

The 680Ω resistors protect the LEDs.

🛠️ Stripboard Build Guide (click to expand)

Step 1 — Identify the parts

555 IC, R1, R2, C1, LEDs, LED resistors, rails.

Step 2 — Place the 555

Always across the centre gap.

Step 3 — Power rails

Red = +9V, Black = 0V.

Step 4 — R1 and R2

R1: pin 8 → pin 7 R2: pin 7 → pins 6/2

Step 5 — Capacitor

C1 connects between pins 2/6 and 0V.

Step 6 — LEDs

Green LED lights on HIGH, red LED lights on LOW.

Step 7 — Add link wires

Used to bridge copper strips.

Important: Stripboard looks different but is electrically identical.

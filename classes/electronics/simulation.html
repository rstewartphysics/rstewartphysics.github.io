This is the Circuit simulation area of the course. You use simulation software to design, construct, test and investigate a circuit's behaviour — and to find faults — before building it for real.

Concept 1

Why simulate before you build?

  • Cheaper & safer — no components are wasted and nothing is damaged while you experiment.
  • Faster — change a value or a connection in seconds and see the effect immediately.
  • Test the design — confirm it behaves as intended, and measure voltages/currents/waveforms with on-screen meters and a virtual oscilloscope.
  • Find faults early — a printout or layout from the simulator shows up mistakes before any soldering.
Practice 1

Give two reasons an engineer simulates a circuit before constructing it.

Answer
Any two of: it avoids wasting/damaging components; it is quicker to change and re-test; faults and design errors are found before building; voltages and waveforms can be measured safely on screen.

Practice 2

Complete the output column for a 2-input NAND gate for inputs (0,0), (0,1), (1,0), (1,1).

Answer
NAND outputs are 1, 1, 1, 0. (It is 0 only when both inputs are 1.)

Concept 4

The half-adder

  • A half-adder adds two single bits A and B and gives a Sum and a Carry.
  • Sum = A XOR B  ·  Carry = A AND B.
  • 1 + 1 = "10" in binary: Sum = 0, Carry = 1.

Practice 4

State the Sum and Carry of a half-adder when A = 1 and B = 1, and name the two gates that produce them.

Answer
Sum = 0, Carry = 1. Sum comes from an XOR gate; Carry comes from an AND gate.

Practice 5

A circuit sounds a buzzer when it gets too hot. Identify a suitable input, process and output sub-system.

Answer
Input: thermistor voltage divider. Process: comparator (switches when the temperature voltage passes a reference) driving a transistor. Output: a buzzer (or relay + buzzer).
Concept 6

Fault-finding from a simulation printout

From a simulated printout or layout diagram you can spot common faults:

  • Wrong supply voltage set on the source.
  • Wrong resistor value (or wrong colour code / BS1852 entered).
  • Wrong orientation of a polarised part — diode, LED, electrolytic capacitor or transistor put in the wrong way round.
Practice 6

A simulated lamp circuit does not light. The printout shows the supply at 9 V and a 470 Ω resistor, but the LED arrow points towards the positive supply. State the fault and the fix.

Answer
The LED is connected the wrong way round (reverse biased), so no current flows and it cannot light. Reverse the LED so its arrow/cathode points towards the negative (0 V) side.

Practice 7

A design needs 5 resistors at £0.05 each, 2 LEDs at £0.12 each and one 555 timer at £0.40. Calculate the total component cost.

Answer
Resistors 5 × £0.05 = £0.25; LEDs 2 × £0.12 = £0.24; 555 = £0.40. Total = £0.25 + £0.24 + £0.40 = £0.89.

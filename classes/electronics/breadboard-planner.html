Four rules that hold on every stripboard

  1. A track is a net. The copper runs the whole way across the board, so every leg in that row is joined to every other leg in it. Turning a circuit diagram into a layout is really just giving each net a track.
  2. Cut every track a chip crosses. A DIL package sits across the tracks, so without a cut between its two rows of pins, pin 1 is joined to pin 14 and pin 7 to pin 8. This is the single most common fault in the verifiers’ reports.
  3. Adjacent pins are not connected. Two pins side by side on a package are on different tracks. Joining them takes a link wire — and every link is numbered and coloured, with red and black kept for +5 V and 0 V.
  4. Every gate input must be held. Pull it down to 0 V through a resistor if a switch or sensor feeds it, tie it straight to 0 V if the gate is spare. An input joined to nothing is not a logic 0 — it floats.

Four rules that hold on every breadboard

  1. A column is a net. The five holes down one column, inside one band, are joined inside the board — a whole rail row is one net too. Turning a circuit diagram into a layout is really just giving each net a column, or a rail.
  2. A chip straddles the channel. A DIL package sits with one row of pins in the a–e band and the other in f–j, so without straddling it, pin 1 is joined to pin 14 and pin 7 to pin 8.
  3. Adjacent pins are not connected. Two pins side by side on a package are in different columns. Joining them takes a jumper wire — and every jumper is numbered and coloured, with red and black kept for +5 V and 0 V.
  4. Every gate input must be held. Pull it down to 0 V through a resistor if a switch or sensor feeds it, tie it straight to 0 V if the gate is spare. An input joined to nothing is not a logic 0 — it floats.

    What the assessment is looking for

    This tool does not give you a mark. It tells you what is joined to what on your board and where that differs from the circuit. The marks for your assignment come from your own hand-drawn layout sheets, judged against the course assessment task descriptors:

    • Detailed component layout diagrams — every component shown as its footprint in the hole it occupies, with its item ID, and every track cut marked.
    • Use of test points — a numbered test point planned on the layout for each sub-system input and output, before you build.
    • Link wires colour-coded and numbered to match your wiring schedule.

    The verifiers' reports flag the same faults every year: no cuts between the two rows of pins of an IC, both legs of a component in one track, circuit symbols drawn instead of footprints, and no test points at all.

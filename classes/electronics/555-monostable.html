Concept 1

What a monostable does

Key words

Monostable
a circuit with one stable state. A trigger gives one pulse, then it goes back.
Trigger
the signal that starts the pulse.
Pulse
the time the output stays high.

A monostable waits with its output low. That is its one stable state.

  • Slide the switch to 0 V and back. This is the trigger.
  • The output goes high. LED1 comes on.
  • After a set time, the output goes low again by itself.

One trigger gives one pulse. The pulse time is set by two parts, R2 and C1. It does not depend on how long you press.

You meet monostables in a stair light that stays on for two minutes. A hand dryer that runs for a set time uses one too.

Practice 1

A monostable gives a 10 s pulse. SW1 is slid to 0 V and back within 1 s.

State how long LED1 stays on. Give a reason.

Answer
10 s. R2 and C1 set the pulse time. A short trigger only starts the pulse.

You can now…

  • say what a monostable does.
  • say what starts the pulse and what sets its length.
Concept 2

The 555 timer's pins

Key words

555
an 8-pin timer chip.
Reset
pin 4. It must go to +5 V, or the chip will not run.
Threshold
pin 6. It ends the pulse when C1 is ⅔ full.
Discharge
pin 7. It empties C1 when the pulse ends.

Hold the chip with the notch at the top. Pin 1 is top left. The pins count down the left side, then up the right side.

  • Pins 8 and 1 power the chip: +5 V and 0 V.
  • Pin 4, reset, goes to +5 V too.
  • Pin 2, trigger: low starts the pulse.
  • Pin 3, output: high during the pulse. It drives LED1.
  • Pins 6 and 7 are joined. R2 and C1 connect here.
  • Pin 5 is not used.
Practice 2

State the pin number for each job.

  • (a) starts the pulse
  • (b) drives LED1
  • (c) must go to +5 V, as well as pin 8
Answer
(a) Pin 2, trigger. (b) Pin 3, output. (c) Pin 4, reset.

You can now…

  • find pin 1 on a 555.
  • name the job of each pin in the monostable.
Concept 3

Read the circuit diagram

Key words

Changeover switch
a slide switch with three pins. The middle pin joins one side or the other.
Pull-up resistor
holds a pin high until something pulls it low. Here, R1 on pin 2.
Electrolytic capacitor
C1. It has a + leg, so it must go the right way round.

This is the circuit in your booklet.

  • SW1 joins pin 2 to +5 V, or to 0 V when you slide it.
  • R1 holds pin 2 high while SW1 is moving and touches neither side.
  • R2 and C1 set the pulse time. They join at pins 6 and 7.
  • R3 protects LED1 by limiting the current.

Test points T1 to T4 are where you put the meter and the probe.

Practice 3

State the job of R3.

Answer
R3 limits the current through LED1, so LED1 is not damaged.

Concept 4

How the pulse starts and ends

Key words

Charge
C1 fills up through R2. The voltage across it rises.
⅔ of the supply
3.3 V on a 5 V supply. When C1 reaches it, the pulse ends.
Describe
give the steps in order. Each step is worth one mark.

Follow the chain from the switch to LED1:

  1. SW1 is slid to 0 V, so pin 2 goes low.
  2. Pin 3 goes high. LED1 comes on.
  3. C1 charges through R2. The voltage across C1 rises.
  4. When C1 reaches ⅔ of the supply, pin 3 goes low. LED1 goes off.
  5. Pin 7 empties C1, ready for the next trigger.

A bigger R2 or a bigger C1 makes C1 fill more slowly. The pulse is longer.

Another way to trigger it

Exam papers often use a push-to-make switch from pin 2 to 0 V. R1 holds pin 2 high. Pressing the switch pulls pin 2 low and starts the pulse.

The rest of the circuit stays the same.

Practice 4

Describe what happens after SW1 is slid to 0 V and back. (3)

Answer
  • Pin 2 goes low, so pin 3 goes high and LED1 comes on (1).
  • C1 charges through R2 (1).
  • When C1 reaches ⅔ of the supply, pin 3 goes low and LED1 goes off (1).

You can now…

  • describe how the pulse starts and ends, one step for each mark.
  • say how R2 and C1 change the pulse.
Concept 5

Calculate the pulse time

Key words

kΩ to Ω
multiply by 1000. 100 kΩ = 100 000 Ω.
µF to F
divide by 1 000 000. 100 µF = 0.000 1 F.
Pulse time of a 555 monostable
T = 1.1 R C
T = pulse time (s) · R = resistance of R2 (Ω) · C = capacitance of C1 (F)
Worked example 1 Our board has R2 = 100 kΩ and C1 = 100 µF. Calculate the pulse time.
T=1.1 R C
T=1.1 × 100 000 × 0.000 1Ω and F
T=11 s
Worked example 2 C1 = 10 µF. A pulse of 1.1 s is needed. Calculate the value of R2.
T=1.1 R C
1.1=1.1 × R × 0.000 0110 µF in F
R=1.1 ÷ (1.1 × 0.000 01)
R=100 000 Ω = 100 kΩ
Practice 5

A monostable has:

  • R2 = 68 kΩ
  • C1 = 47 µF

(a) Calculate the pulse time.

(b) State the new pulse time if C1 is changed to 470 µF.

Answer

(a) T = 1.1 × 68 000 × 0.000 047 = 3.5 s

(b) C1 is 10 times bigger, so the pulse is 10 times longer: 35 s.

You can now…

  • calculate the pulse time from R2 and C1.
  • change kΩ to Ω and µF to F.

Concept 6

Simulate it in Yenka

Key words

Voltmeter
measures the voltage across C1. Connect it from C1's + leg to 0 V.
Logic indicator
shows the logic level. In Yenka, red = high and white = low.

This is booklet Task 1. Build the circuit in Yenka first, then add:

  • a voltmeter across C1
  • a logic indicator on pin 3

Slide SW1 to 0 V and straight back. Time how long the logic indicator stays red. Watch the voltmeter climb while it is red.

Practice 6

In Yenka, the logic indicator is red. The voltmeter across C1 reads 1.2 V.

State whether the pulse has ended. Give a reason.

Answer
No. C1 has not reached ⅔ of the supply (3.3 V) yet, so pin 3 is still high.

  • add a voltmeter and a logic indicator to a Yenka circuit.
  • time a pulse in Yenka.
Concept 7

Build it on breadboard

This is booklet Tasks 2 to 4. The worked guide takes you from the diagram to the breadboard, one net at a time. A net is a set of legs that are joined. Then find the mistake on three faulty boards.

Pre-power-up check

Do this before you connect the supply. Tick each one in booklet Task 4.

  1. No beep between T1 and T2 on continuity: no short across the supply.
  2. IC1 straddles the centre gap, notch and pin 1 on the left.
  3. C1 the right way round: + leg to pins 6 and 7, − leg to 0 V.
  4. LED1 the right way round: long leg to R3, short leg to 0 V.
  5. Every wire matches the wiring schedule.
  6. The supply is set to 5 V before it is connected.
Practice 7

Give two checks you would make on C1 before you connect the power.

Answer
Any two: the value is 100 µF; the + leg goes to pins 6 and 7; the − leg (the stripe) goes to 0 V; its legs are in the right columns.

  • do a pre-power-up check.

Compare each reading with the one you expect. The first one that is wrong tells you where to look.

  • T1 not 5 V: look at the supply leads.
  • T3 never goes to 0 V: look at SW1 and its wires to pin 2 and 0 V.

Practice 8

LED1 never lights. T1 reads 5.0 V. T3 goes to 0 V when SW1 is slid. T4 reads 3.6 V for 11 s.

Suggest the fault.

Answer
The 555 is working: pin 3 goes high. The fault is after pin 3. LED1 is the wrong way round, or R3 or LED1 is not joined.

Booklet answers: Lesson 3

Try the task in your booklet first. Then tap to check.

Task 1: Yenka
  • R2 = 100 kΩ: pulse about 11 s. C1 reads about 3.3 V (⅔ of 5 V) when it ends.
  • R2 = 47 kΩ: pulse about 5.2 s. C1 still reads about 3.3 V when it ends.
Task 2: Plan it
Check your plan against Part 2 of the worked guide above. Every net needs its own column or rail, and IC1 straddles the centre gap.
Task 5: Test it
Why your average might not be exactly 11 s: capacitor tolerance (often ±20 %), resistor tolerance, or your reaction time.
Task 6: Fault from readings
  • (a) Pin 2 never goes low. SW1 is wired wrongly or not joined to pin 2, or its 0 V pin is not connected.
  • (b) Any two: SW1 left at 0 V. A wire or a leg joins pin 2's column to 0 V. SW1 is one column out, so its 0 V pin sits in pin 2's column.
Exam question 1 (2025 Q11)
  • Rails labelled, with nodes (1).
  • Pins 4 and 8 to +6 V, pin 1 to 0 V (1).
  • SW1 and R1 in series between the rails (1).
  • R2 and C1 in series, C1 the right way round (1).
  • Pins 6, 7 and 2 joined, pin 5 not connected, Voutput from pin 3 with a node (1).
  • Symbols and labels (1).
Exam question 2 (2025 Q7)
  • (a)(i) Red wire to the +V supply, black wire to 0 V.
  • (a)(ii) Place the probe tip on the point (1). The red LED lights (1).
  • (b)(i) T3 and T4.
  • (b)(ii) Pin 7 is connected to 0 V (ground).
Exam question 3: three errors
  • C1 is the wrong way round.
  • The LED is the wrong way round.
  • Pin 4 (reset) is connected to 0 V. It must go to +5 V.

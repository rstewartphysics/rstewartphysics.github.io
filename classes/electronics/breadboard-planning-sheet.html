Breadboard planning sheet

Printed at true size — 0.1 inch between holes and 0.3 inch across the centre channel — so you can lay a real breadboard on top and the a–j holes line up. Print at 100%, not “fit to page”, then check the bar below with a ruler before you draw on it.

Plan your own breadboard circuit

Check the scale first

Measure the bar with a ruler. If it is not exactly 50 mm, the print scaling is wrong and the grid will not match a real board — print again at 100%.

Half board — 400 points, 30 columns

The five holes down each column, a–e or f–j, are joined inside the board; each rail is joined all the way along. How far the rails sit from row a and row j differs between makes, so line a real board up on the a–j holes.

Before you build it

  • Every part has an item ID, the same on the layout and the schedule.
  • Every chip straddles the centre channel, one row of pins in a–e and the other in f–j, pin 1 marked.
  • No part has two legs in the same five-hole column.
  • One hole, one thing: every leg and every jumper end in a hole of its own.
  • No jumper runs straight across a chip — it goes round the end.
  • Every chip powered: its supply and 0 V pins wired to the rails.
  • If the bottom rails are used, they are joined to the top ones.
  • Every jumper numbered and coloured; red and black used for nothing but the supply.
  • Every gate input held down to 0 V unless another gate drives it.
  • Polarised parts the right way round.

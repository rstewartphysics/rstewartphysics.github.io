Getting started on the Uno

Our boards are Elegoo Uno R3, a copy of the Arduino Uno. You program them in Duino.app.

  1. Open Google Chrome (or Edge) and go to duino.app. Safari and Firefox can't reach the USB port.
  2. Plug the board in with its USB cable. A light on the board comes on.
  3. Choose the board Arduino Uno. Check it every lesson: the wrong board means the upload fails.
  4. The first time you upload, Chrome asks which USB device to use. Pick the one that names Arduino, CH340 or USB Serial, then Connect.
  5. In the Code tab, delete the code that is already there, then type yours.

Verify before you Upload, every time. Verify (some versions say Compile) checks your spelling and punctuation without touching the board. Upload sends the program to the board and runs it. If Verify shows an error, it names the line: fix it first.

Anything after // is a note for people. The board ignores it. Delete the code and the board stops working; delete the notes and it works the same, but no one can follow it. Add your own notes to explain your thinking.

LEDs and buzzers on the breadboard

  • Every LED needs a 220 Ω resistor in series. Without one, too much current burns out the LED, and can damage the pin.
  • The LED's long leg (+) goes towards the Uno's pin. The short leg, by the flat edge, goes through the resistor to GND.
  • An LED works only one way round. If it won't light, turn it round.
  • A resistor has no + or −. 220 Ω is red, red, brown, then gold.
  • A buzzer needs no resistor. Its + leg (marked +, longer) goes to the pin.

It won't upload, or it won't work

What happensCheck this
Upload fails, or "no board"Arduino Uno chosen? USB plugged in? Did you press Connect when Chrome asked?
Red error on VerifyRead the line number. Usually a missing ; or a misspelled word.
Uploaded, but nothing happensDid you press Upload, or only Verify? Verify only checks.
Nothing on the breadboard works5V to the + rail and GND to the − rail? Legs in the same row, on the same side of the gap?
One LED won't lightLong leg towards the pin? Its resistor reaching GND? Try it the other way round.
